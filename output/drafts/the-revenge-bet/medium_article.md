# The Revenge Bet

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. This is mistake #7.*

---

The bathroom light in that apartment was one of those flickery fluorescent tubes that made everyone look like they had jaundice. I remember the light because that's what I stared at while I refreshed the app for the fourteenth time, waiting for a 3rd-string tight end I'd never heard of to catch a pass in a Wednesday night game I had no business watching.

I had $200 in the account on Monday morning.

By Wednesday at 10:47pm, I had $50. And I was about to tap the button that would take it to zero.

Here's the part nobody tells you about revenge bets. You don't feel like a gambler. You feel like an accountant. You're not chasing thrills, you're chasing a specific number. The number you had on Monday. The number that would make it like Monday never happened.

Monday I lost $80 on a parlay a coworker sent me. Guy named Dwayne who chews his pens and thinks he's connected because his cousin bartends near a stadium. The parlay had four legs. Three hit. The fourth was a backup quarterback who threw an interception on the first drive and got benched. I remember laughing when it happened. Actual laughing. That should have been the sign.

The sign that I was calm meant I hadn't started chasing yet.

Tuesday I was fine. Tuesday I went to work, ate a sad turkey wrap at my desk, watched a YouTube video about a guy who restores old radios, and went to bed at a reasonable hour. Tuesday was the version of me I keep trying to be.

Wednesday I opened the account at lunch. Just to look. That's always the lie. Just to look.

By 6pm I had a bet on a college basketball game in a conference I couldn't have located on a map. By 8pm I was down another $70 and telling myself I just needed one to hit. By 9:30 I was in a live-bet spiral, tapping props on players whose names I was reading for the first time as I bet on them.

I wasn't doing analysis. I was doing math. If this hits at +180 I'm back to $140. If then I put $100 on the next one at -110 I'm at $230, so I'm up $30 from Monday, so it never happened.

That's what revenge betting is. It's a guy with a spreadsheet trying to erase a Monday.

At 10:47pm I had $50 and one game left. A late West Coast thing. I remember I could hear my upstairs neighbor's dog walking across the floor, those little clicks. I remember I hadn't eaten dinner. I remember I had a work presentation the next morning that I hadn't looked at.

And I remember thinking, very clearly, with the calm of a man who has already lost: if I put the whole $50 on this, and it hits, I'm back to almost even.

I tapped the button.

What happened next is the part I've never told anyone in this much detail. Not my brother. Not the woman I was seeing at the time. Not the therapist I saw twice and quit because he asked good questions.

I wrote it all down. Every stupid tap. Every rationalization. The thing my brain did around 9pm that I now recognize as the exact same thing my brain does before every other bad decision I've ever made.
