# The Second Bet Was Worse Than The First

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 5 of 943 (part 2 of 7).*

The morning after I lost the $2,400, I woke up at 4:11 AM and opened the account before I opened my eyes.

The morning after I lost the $2,400, I woke up at 4:11 AM and opened the account before I opened my eyes.

I know it was 4:11 because there's a screenshot. I take screenshots of things when I'm in a bad state. It's a habit I picked up during my second divorce, from a therapist who suggested I document my thoughts. What she meant was journaling. What I do is take pictures of my phone screen at ungodly hours so that later, when I'm lying to myself about what happened, I can produce evidence.

At 4:11 AM, according to the screenshot, my account balance was $6,847.

At 4:14 AM I placed a bet for $1,800 on a college football market that wasn't going to resolve for another four days.

I want to walk you through what was going on in my head, because I remember it clearly, and it's the kind of thing you only remember clearly when it's shameful.

I told myself I was being disciplined. That was the word. Disciplined. Because unlike yesterday — yesterday, the disaster — I had done research. Research meant I had spent forty minutes on a subreddit reading posts by people who sounded like they knew things. Research meant I had looked at the two platforms and confirmed that this time, they agreed. Both around 62 cents. Consensus. Real market. No soft open. I had learned my lesson.

Here's what I hadn't learned.

I hadn't learned that the reason I was on the account at 4:11 AM was not that I had discovered an opportunity. The reason I was on the account at 4:11 AM was that I had lost $2,400 the day before and my body would not let me sleep until I had done something about it.

That is not discipline. That is the opposite of discipline. That is what discipline looks like in a Halloween costume.

I placed the bet. $1,800. I put the phone face down. I went back to bed. I did not go back to sleep. I lay in the dark and did the math on what I'd have if I was right — $2,900, roughly — and how that would put me back above where I started the week, and how the whole $2,400 disaster would become a story I told at a bar someday, one of those "can you believe I almost quit" stories, instead of what it actually was, which was the beginning of something.

I want to tell you what my brother said to me on the phone that night, but I'll save it for next time because it deserves its own space. What I'll tell you now is that between 4:14 AM Wednesday and 9:00 PM Sunday, when the market resolved, I checked the app 71 times. I know because the app tells you. There's a little usage stat buried in the settings. 71 times in four days.

The market resolved against me.

Not catastrophically. I got 22 cents back on the dollar because of some technicality about how the platform handles partial outcomes. I still don't fully understand it. What I understand is that $1,800 went in and $396 came out and the difference — $1,404 — was gone in a way that felt different from the first loss.

The first loss felt like tripping.

This one felt like walking.

And here's the thing I need you to understand, because it's the whole point of this post: I already knew. On Wednesday morning, at 4:11 AM, when I opened the account before I opened my eyes, I already knew what I was. I just didn't have the vocabulary for it yet. Or I did have the vocabulary and I was refusing to use it, which is the same thing wearing a different hat.

The screenshot is still on my phone. I looked at it before I wrote this.
