# The Wednesday I Made Eggs Instead

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 4 of 943 (part 2 of 7).*

The line moved a full point between when I woke up and when the eggs were done.

The line moved a full point between when I woke up and when the eggs were done.

I didn't know that then. I know it now because I went back and looked at the historical line data, which is a thing you can do for free, which is a thing I could have done any time all week. On Wednesday at 6:47 a.m., which is roughly when I was cracking two eggs into a nonstick pan, the home team was minus 2. By 7:15, when I sat down to eat, it was minus 2 and dropping.

My team was leaking value while I salted my breakfast.

But I want to describe the Wednesday morning honestly, because this is where the arc actually happens. Not Tuesday night when I placed the bet. Not Sunday afternoon when I lost it. Wednesday morning. That was the day I had every chance to catch it, and instead I did something that felt more responsible than looking at my phone.

I made breakfast.

Here is the small self-portrait. Kitchen light on, the yellow one over the sink because the overhead was still too bright for 6:45. Coffee already gurgling. I had my reading glasses pushed up on my forehead because I was going to check the paper — actual paper, delivered, don't laugh — after I ate. The dog was staring at me from her spot by the fridge doing the guilt thing.

My phone was on the counter, face down. I remember this specifically because I remember thinking, look at me. Look at me being present. Look at me not being one of these guys who wakes up and immediately doomscrolls. My wife had been on me about phone-first mornings and I had decided, sometime in the last week, to be better about it.

So I made the eggs. I toasted the bread. I ate. I read the front page and the sports section. I let the dog out. I let the dog in. I put the plate in the dishwasher. I did all of this in the specific self-congratulatory mode of a man who thinks not looking at his phone for thirty minutes is a personality upgrade.

And the line kept moving.

Here's what I want to say and it's going to sound like I'm being hard on myself but I'm not, I'm just being accurate. There is a version of me — a version that exists in every other functional adult I know — that would have, at some point during that Wednesday morning, opened the book for ten seconds. Not to bet. Not to obsess. Just to see. Just to confirm the world hadn't moved.

But I had put the phone face down. And in my head, checking the app would have violated the tiny virtue I had built for myself that morning. It would have been backsliding. It would have been the phone-first behavior my wife had complained about.

So I made the mistake dressed as a good habit.

That's the part I keep chewing on. Not that I didn't check. That I didn't check because I was performing wellness. I traded ninety seconds of information for the story of being a man who eats breakfast without a screen.

I got in the car at 7:40. Drove to work. Radio, not phone. Another small virtue. Got to the office at 8:05. Coffee, small talk, opened my laptop, opened email, opened a spreadsheet, opened another spreadsheet. Never once, in any of those app-openings, did I open the book.

By noon on Wednesday the line had settled at minus 2. That's a full point of movement in eighteen hours. In sharp-money language that's the market shouting.

I was in a meeting about Q3 targets.

I want to tell you I felt something in the back of my mind, some tug, some suspicion. I didn't. That's the honest part. The bet had left my mind entirely by lunch. I had done the hard work Tuesday. That was the whole point of doing it Tuesday.

Wednesday, in my head, was a day off.

Wednesday, in reality, was the day the market started to tell me I was wrong. And I was making eggs. And I was proud of the eggs.

*By Friday I actually did open the account. And I still didn't see it. Wait until you hear why.*
