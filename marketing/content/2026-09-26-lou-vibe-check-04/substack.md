# The Night I Loaded The Card

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 8 of 943 (part 4 of 7).*

The number I'm not comfortable saying is $1,140.

The number I'm not comfortable saying is $1,140.

That's what I was down, cumulative, on the evening of October 8th. Twenty-two days after the EdgeLord post. I'd made eleven more bets since the Fed contract. Some had won. Most had not. The winners were small. The losers were not. That's how it works, in case you were wondering. That's how it always works.

On the evening of October 8th I was in my basement office. Which is not really an office. It's a corner of the basement with a desk from my old job that I never returned. There's a window at ground level that looks out onto the side yard where the neighbor's cat comes to eat the birds I feed. I was watching the cat. It was watching the feeder. We were both waiting for something to die.

I opened my banking app.

I had, in checking, $1,847. That was for the mortgage on the first, which was twenty-three days away, and groceries, and my wife's dentist copay, and the electric. That $1,847 was not extra money. That $1,847 was the money.

I stared at the number.

I opened the account. There was a contract I'd been watching all day. Election-related. I don't want to be more specific because it doesn't matter. What matters is that I had a thesis, or I thought I had a thesis, and the thesis had come to me at 3pm while I was making a sandwich and I had spent the intervening five hours convincing myself the thesis was correct. This is what a thesis is, at 55, in a basement, when you're down $1,140. It is a story you tell yourself to justify the thing you already want to do.

I wanted to bet $500.

I did the math three times. If it hit, I'd make about $340. If it lost, I'd be down $1,640 total for the month, which was more than I'd ever lost on anything in my life outside of that car in 2003 that I've written about before.

I opened the the book deposit page. I typed 500. I stared at it.

Then I typed over it. I typed 800.

I want to walk you through the eight seconds between typing 500 and typing 800 because those eight seconds are the whole story. In those eight seconds, my brain did a math that I don't think I could reconstruct if you paid me. Something like: if I'm going to risk anything I might as well risk enough to actually get out of the hole; if I bet 500 and win I'm still down 800; if I bet 800 and win I'm almost back to even; and if I bet 800 and lose, well, I'll cross that bridge; and I'm not going to lose because the thesis is good; and even if the thesis is wrong, the contract price implies a probability that doesn't match my read of the situation; and I am, when you get right down to it, smart, and I have been smart my whole life, and it's time I got paid for being smart instead of pretending I'm not.

That last part. That's the part that scares me now.

I typed 800. I hit deposit. It came out of the checking. It came out of the mortgage money. I did it. I actually did it.

I put the full $800 on the contract at 62 cents. My cursor hovered over confirm for maybe twelve seconds. I could hear the furnace kick on. The cat outside had left. The feeder was empty.

I clicked.

And here's the part I'm going to tell you because if I don't tell you it then this whole newsletter is a lie. I felt euphoric. For about ninety seconds, I felt better than I had felt in months. Because I had done something. I had taken action. I had bet on myself. I had, in some deranged corner of my head, become the sharp.

The ninety seconds ended.

The contract dropped to 58 cents. Then 54. Then 49. Within an hour it was at 41. I was down roughly $270 on the position and $1,410 for the month and $1,410 out of the money that was supposed to pay the mortgage.

I did not close.

I went upstairs. My wife was at the kitchen table doing a crossword. She looked up. She said, 'You okay? You look pale.' I said the basement was cold. Fourth lie? Fifth? I'd lost count.

I didn't sleep that night. I lay on my back and stared at the ceiling fan and made deals with a god I don't believe in. Give it back to 62 and I'll never bet again. Give it back to 55 and I'll close and take the loss and walk away. Give it back to 50 and I'll at least sleep.

At 4am I checked. It was at 38.

At 6am my wife rolled over and asked if I'd slept and I said 'a little.' She kissed my shoulder and got up. The shoulder kiss is what got me. The shoulder kiss from a woman who had no idea I had put the mortgage on a screen in the basement.

The contract closed the next afternoon at 19 cents.

I lost $344 on that bet.

Total for the arc, at that point: $1,484.
