# The Crawl Out And Every Coward Move In It

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 8 of 943 (part 5 of 7).*

I want to be honest about the crawl out because most of what you read online about people quitting bad habits is written after the fact by people who want to sound heroic. I don't want to sound heroic. I want to tell you what actually happened so that if you're in it, you'll recognize yourself, and if you're not, you'll be less impressed with the people who claim they quit clean.

The morning after the $344 loss, October 10th, I sat at the kitchen table before my wife was up. I had a spiral notebook. The kind with the wire on top that catches on your sleeve. I wrote at the top: STOP.

I underlined it twice.

I then, and I swear this is true, opened the account and made another bet. $60. On a weather contract. I don't remember why. I remember thinking that if I could win this one small bet it would prove I still had judgment, and if I still had judgment then the previous night wasn't as bad as it looked.

Coward move number one: betting to prove I could quit.

The weather contract won. I made $34. I felt, for about six minutes, that I had turned a corner. Then I made a second bet. $80. On something else. I don't remember what.

Coward move number two: mistaking a lucky win for a signal from the universe.

I lost the $80.

Coward move number three: I did not tell my wife any of this. Not the $344, not the $60, not the $80. I sat across from her at dinner that night while she talked about the plumber coming Thursday and I ate meatloaf and I made appropriate noises and I did not say a single true thing about my day.

October 11th. My wife's sister called. There was some family thing. I don't remember. I wasn't listening. I was calculating, in the background of my own head, whether I could put another $200 down and be back to even by the 20th. The math worked if I hit 62% of my bets at 1.4x average odds. I know because I did the math on a napkin at the coffee shop while she was on the phone.

Coward move number four: doing gambling math in a coffee shop next to my wife on a family phone call.

October 12th. I made my last bet. I want to say I stopped because I had a moment of clarity. I did not. I stopped because I logged into the account and saw my account balance and it was $47 and I did not have the emotional energy to fund it again. That's it. That's the whole moment of transformation. I was tired and there wasn't enough money in the account to matter.

I closed the browser tab.

Coward move number five: I did not close the account. I did not delete the account. I did not remove the bookmark. I just closed the tab.

October 15th. I finally told my wife. Not all of it. About 60% of it. I told her I had lost 'around $800' on the books. The real number was $1,528. I picked 800 because 800 sounded bad but survivable. I picked 800 because I was a coward. She was quiet for a long time. Then she said, 'Is that all?' And I said, 'Yes.' And she looked at me the way she looks at me when she doesn't believe me but has decided not to fight about it right now.

Coward move number six: shaving a thousand dollars off the truth because I could not stand to say the real number to a woman who married me.

October 20th. I told her the real number. I had to, because the credit card statement was going to show the the book deposits and she was going to see them. I told her at breakfast. She put down her fork. She said, 'Okay.' Then she said, 'I need a minute,' and she went to the bedroom and closed the door for eighteen minutes. I sat at the table and listened to the fridge cycle.

When she came out her eyes were red. She sat down. She said, 'What are we doing.' Not a question. A statement. What are we doing.

I didn't have an answer.

Coward move number seven: I still did not close the account with the book that day. I closed it four days later. I told her it took me four days to figure out how to close it. That was true and also a lie. It took me four days because I wanted to keep the option of coming back. I wanted the tab available. I wanted the door not fully shut. I did not admit this to her or to myself until about two weeks later when I wrote it down in the same spiral notebook that said STOP at the top.

Coward move number eight, and the worst one: for the first three weeks after closing the account, when I felt bad, or bored, or restless, or unseen, I opened message board and searched EdgeLord_Alpha's handle and read his tweets. I read his tweets. I did not follow him. I did not reply. I read his tweets like a person keeping tabs on an ex. I don't fully understand why. I think part of me wanted him to be right, later, about something big, so that at least I could say I had known someone who was right about something once.

He was mostly wrong. But he kept posting. He always kept posting.

I finally blocked him on November 8th. Fifty-three days after the original post.

That's the crawl out. Eight coward moves and one block button.
