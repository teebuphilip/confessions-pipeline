# The Number In Red

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 8 of 943 (part 2 of 7).*

I checked my phone before I peed.

I checked my phone before I peed.

That's the sentence I want you to remember. That's the sentence that tells you everything about what kind of man I was on the morning of September 17th.

6:04am. Still dark. My wife was breathing that slow deep breath she does when she's about to wake up on her own in twelve minutes. The dog was on the rug at the foot of the bed doing that leg-twitch thing dogs do when they're dreaming about running.

And I reached for the phone.

Not for the alarm. The alarm wasn't set. Not for a message. Nobody messages me at 6am. I reached for the phone because at 11:47pm the night before I had put $300 into a contract based on a post from a stranger, and the very first thought in my conscious brain, before language, before 'good morning,' before the awareness that I had a bladder, was: what's it at.

What's it at.

I opened the account in the dark. The screen was too bright. I squinted like a man defusing a bomb.

The number was red.

Not a little red. A lot of red. The contract had opened Monday morning down to 11 cents. I had bought at 42. Basic math, which I was doing badly at 6am in the dark with my thumb, told me I was down roughly 74%. Of $300. Which meant I had, at that moment, $78 worth of contract left.

I had lost $222 in my sleep.

I want to describe what I felt because it wasn't what you'd expect. It wasn't a punch. It wasn't a hot flash of shame. It was quieter than that. It was the feeling of realizing you left the oven on and drove to work. A cold small dawning. A little door opening in the back of your head that says: you did this. Nobody else. You. In the dark. Alone. With a laptop.

I got up. Peed. Sat on the toilet longer than I needed to because sitting on the toilet is one of the few places a married man can hide legally. I refreshed the app. It was now down to 9 cents. Another six dollars gone in the four minutes I'd been awake.

I closed the account. I stared at the tile floor.

I thought about EdgeLord_Alpha. I went back to the board. I wanted to see what he'd said. Surely he'd said something. Surely there was a follow-up. Surely a man with 4,200 followers who called himself sharp would post something on a Monday morning when his call had cratered.

He had posted. He had posted three times.

First post, 5:12am: 'Rough open. Model still likes it. Adding.'

Second post, 5:47am: 'This is why size matters. If you can't handle drawdown, don't tail.'

Third post, 6:01am: A meme. An actual meme. A frog in sunglasses.

I sat on the toilet reading a frog meme from a man who had just cost me $222 in six hours and I felt, for the first time, the specific taste of what it is to be a mark. Not a victim. A mark. There's a difference. A victim is chosen. A mark chooses himself. A mark walks into the room with his wallet already open and asks who wants to help him hold it.

I was the mark.

I did not tell my wife. I made coffee. I made her toast. I asked how she slept. She said fine. I said me too. Second lie of the arc. There would be more.

And then, and this is the part that still bothers me, I went back to my laptop at 7:15am and I did not sell. I held. Because a mark, once he knows he's a mark, does not stop being a mark. He upgrades to hoping.

The contract went to zero by noon.
