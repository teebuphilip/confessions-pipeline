# What I Should Have Done: The Rules I Use Now

*A note before we start: if this feels like you've missed something, you probably have. Life got in the way — again, as it does — and some of these ran out of order. You can find the earlier posts in the archive. Lou doesn't always do things in sequence. Neither does life.*


*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 3 of 943 (part 6 of 7).*

Inspiration is what got me here. Inspiration is the guy on a message board with the megaphone emoji telling me to trust my gut. Inspiration is minus 3.5 feeling right at 9am on a Tuesday. I don't trust inspiration anymore. I trust checklists. Checklists are boring. Boring is what saved me.

So here are the actual things I do. If you take one and use it, great. If you take none, I don't care. This isn't a framework. It's a list of specific things a specific idiot has to do to stay out of trouble.

Before any bet, I open three tabs. Not two. Three. The book I'm going to bet at. the book. another shop. I look at all three. If the book's line disagrees with the the books by more than a point, I don't bet. I don't try to figure out who's right. I don't tell myself the sharps are on my side. I don't bet. That's it. That's the whole rule. Disagreement equals no bet.

I check the timestamp. If the line moved in the last two hours and I didn't know why before I opened the account, I don't bet. Line movement I can't explain is money someone else has that I don't. This rule alone would have saved me the original $400 in August. This rule alone.

I have a weekly cap. It's $80. Eighty dollars. Not a hundred. Eighty. The odd number is on purpose. Round numbers are for people who round. If I hit eighty in losses in a given week, I'm done until the next Monday. I don't chase. I don't 'get it back.' I take the L and I close the account. This has cost me the fun of a comeback exactly zero times because comebacks aren't real.

I don't bet on Sunday morning. I used to. Sunday morning is when the whole population of America is betting, so the lines are the softest they'll ever be, which is exactly why I don't do it. If it feels too easy, I'm the mark. If everyone at the table is smiling, I'm the meal.

I don't watch the game if I have money on it. This one was hard. This one took six months. But watching the game while you have a bet down is not watching the game. It's watching your own nervous system. The bet ruins the game and the game ruins your evening. I put the phone in the drawer with the takeout menus, the same drawer she put the folder in, and I don't check the score until it's final. Sometimes I know the result before I look because the house is either quiet or I've been muttering.

Everything hits the joint account. Every deposit, every withdrawal. No separate accounts. No 'entertainment' cards. No fun money in the sock drawer. If my wife can't see it in real time, I can't do it. This was her rule, not mine, and it's the best rule in the list.

I tell Diane every bet. Every one. Even the small ones. Especially the small ones. The small ones are the tell. If I want to lie to Diane about a $20 bet, I have already lost. The lying is the disease. The bet is the fever.

Once a month I do the total. Not just wins. Not just losses. The total. Deposits minus withdrawals since day one. I write it in a notebook. Actual paper notebook. Because the notebook doesn't scroll. When you scroll, you can pretend the earlier months didn't happen. The notebook remembers.

Current total, since August of last year, is minus $612.

Minus $612. I have made 187 bets. I have watched hundreds of hours of games. I have set 4am alarms. I have hidden folders. I have lied to my wife over eggs. I have paid a therapist $170 to tell me I wasn't ready to see her. I have almost lost my marriage.

For minus $612.

That number should be tattooed on the inside of my eyelids.

One more rule. If I feel excited about a bet, I wait an hour. Just an hour. If it still looks good in an hour, I bet. If it doesn't, I don't. Excitement is a smell. It smells like the guy who lost the previous $400. I don't trust him. I don't invite him to dinner. When I smell him in the room, I open a window and I wait.

That's the list. Nine things. None of them are wisdom. All of them are just fences I've built around the version of me that will absolutely, given the chance, walk off a cliff for a $180 payout on a Thursday night hockey game between two teams he cannot name.

Tomorrow: what all of this still costs me, even now.

*The rules work. That doesn't mean the damage stops.*

— Lou
