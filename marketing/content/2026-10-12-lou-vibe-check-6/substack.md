# What I Should Have Done: The Rules In The Spiral Notebook

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 8 of 943 (part 6 of 7).*

I wrote these in a spiral notebook on October 24th at the kitchen table with a cheap Bic pen.

I wrote these in a spiral notebook on October 24th at the kitchen table with a cheap Bic pen. My wife was at Pilates. The dog was under my chair. I had a cup of coffee I wasn't drinking. This is what I wrote. I'm giving them to you exactly. No polish. No framework. No metaphors.

Rule 1. Nobody on a message board is sharp. Not the ones with blue checks. Not the ones with 40,000 followers. Not the ones who post charts. Not the ones with track records they curated themselves. If they were sharp they would not be posting about it for free. They would be sharp quietly, somewhere else, with money you will never see.

Rule 2. If I cannot explain why a market is mispriced in three sentences, using my own words, without googling, I do not bet on it. This is a hard rule. This is not a suggestion. This has caught me seven times since I wrote it.

Rule 3. I keep my own spreadsheet. Every bet. Date. Contract. My stated reason. Amount. Result. If I don't have time to type it into the spreadsheet before placing the bet, I don't have time to make the bet.

Rule 4. The spreadsheet must have a column called 'source.' If the source is anyone I don't know personally, in real life, whose track record I have not verified with my own eyes across at least twenty prior calls, the bet doesn't get placed.

Rule 5. Nobody I know personally in real life is doing this well. So Rule 4, in practice, means I don't tail anyone. Ever. This is the point of Rule 4.

Rule 6. Maximum bet size: $50. Not a percentage. Not a Kelly formula. A number. Fifty dollars. Every time I've tried to use a formula I've talked myself into a bigger number. Fifty is fifty. Fifty is not a mortgage payment. Fifty is not a plumber. Fifty is a dinner out.

Rule 7. Deposits are capped at $200 per month. If I lose the $200 I stop. I do not top up. I do not use another account. I do not use a different platform. I stop.

Rule 8. I do not check the app before 9am or after 9pm. The morning check and the late-night check were the two places I made my worst decisions. If the contract moves between 9pm and 9am, it moves without me.

Rule 9. My wife has read-only access to the account. I gave her the login on October 26th. She checks it maybe once a month. She has never said anything about it. The point is not that she polices me. The point is that I cannot pretend it's private.

Rule 10. Every bet gets a written reason before it is placed. Handwritten. In the notebook. If I am not willing to hand-write a paragraph explaining my thesis, I am not going to bet. This has stopped me more than anything else. It turns out most of my urges to bet cannot survive the search for a pen.

Rule 11. If I break any of Rules 1-10, I write it down. I do not hide it. The rule is not 'don't break the rules.' The rule is 'don't hide breaking the rules.' Hiding is worse than losing.

Rule 12. When I feel the urge to bet outside these rules, I walk to the mailbox. Round trip is four minutes. About 80% of the urge dies in those four minutes. The other 20% I have to deal with, but starting from the mailbox I usually deal with it better than starting from the desk.

Rule 13. I do not read gambling message board. Not to be inspired. Not to be warned. Not to laugh at the losers. Not to admire the winners. I do not read it. I have muted 47 accounts as of this writing.

Rule 14. I do not tell people I have rules. If I tell people I have rules, I start to perform them, and when I'm performing them I'm not living them. This one contradicts itself because I'm writing them down for you. I'm aware. The exception is: I can tell people in a specific written context where the point is honesty, not performance. This newsletter counts. My drinking buddy does not.

Rule 15. Every three months I look at the spreadsheet and I add up the totals. If I'm down, I cut the monthly deposit cap in half. If I'm down again the next quarter, I cut it in half again. If I hit zero, I stop for a year.

Those are the rules. Fifteen of them. In a Bic-pen scrawl in a spiral notebook that is currently in the drawer under the toaster oven. I look at them, on average, twice a week. I have broken them, at last count, four times since October 24th. Each of the four times is written in the notebook with a date and a description. This is the part that matters most. Not the not-breaking. The writing-it-down when I break.

I have made $18 net since October 24th. Eighteen dollars. Across roughly forty small bets. That is not a good return. That is not what I'm here for. I'm here to not lose $1,528 again.

*Tomorrow: what this still costs me at breakfast, in the quiet, when nobody is asking.*

— Lou
