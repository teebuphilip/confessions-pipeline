# The Tail: What It Still Costs Me At Breakfast

*A note before we start: if this feels like you've missed something, you probably have. Life got in the way — again, as it does — and some of these ran out of order. You can find the earlier posts in the archive. Lou doesn't always do things in sequence. Neither does life.*


*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 8 of 943 (part 7 of 7).*

It's been four months and my wife still glances at my phone when I check it in the morning.

It's been four months and my wife still glances at my phone when I check it in the morning.

She doesn't say anything. She doesn't ask. She just glances. A little flick of the eyes, off her coffee cup, over to my hand, and back. It takes maybe half a second. She's not doing it to hurt me. She's doing it because for eight days in October I lied to her at breakfast about what I was doing on the phone, and now her nervous system files 'phone in the morning' under 'thing to keep an eye on.'

That's the tail. That's what it still costs me. Not the money. The money's gone and mostly forgotten. The mortgage got paid. The credit card got paid. We moved on financially. What we did not move on from is the half-second glance.

I thought about hiding this post. I thought about not writing it. Because in the arc of a good confession you're supposed to end with something. A lesson. A shape. A little rise. And what I have is a woman glancing at a phone.

Here are the other things it still costs me.

I don't watch football with the sound on anymore. Not because of the football. Because of the commercials. Every third ad is a book. Every third ad has some guy in a sharp suit saying 'lock' or 'edge' or 'sharp' and my whole body does a little thing. Not a big thing. A little thing. The way a dog does when the doorbell rings on TV.

My brother-in-law Dane still tries to talk to me about the books at family gatherings. I told him once, at a Sunday dinner at his house, that his 'sharp' had cost me $300 and had no P&L to speak of. He got red. He hasn't fully forgiven me. His wife told my wife that I embarrassed him. My wife told her sister that maybe Dane needed embarrassing. That created something between the sisters that hasn't quite healed. So there's a wedge in a family, running through a Sunday dinner, that started with a post by a man I have never met.

I still, sometimes, at maybe 10pm on a bored Tuesday, feel the pull. I don't act on it. But feeling it is its own small tax. There's a version of my brain that will never fully believe I don't have edge. That version of my brain has been quieted, not killed. It sits in the back of the room. It raises its hand occasionally.

I look at my phone less. This sounds like a benefit but it isn't, entirely. I look at my phone less because I don't fully trust myself with it. I have removed apps I used to enjoy because they were adjacent to the ones that hurt me. I no longer read certain writers I used to like because their audiences overlap with gambling audiences and I don't want the recommendations. My internet has gotten smaller. Some of that is good. Some of it is just smaller.

My spiral notebook is still in the drawer under the toaster oven. I don't need it there. It could go anywhere. But it lives there because that's where I put it in October and moving it feels like declaring victory, and I am not declaring victory. I am, at 55, four months in, cautiously optimistic that I have learned something. I have been cautiously optimistic before. The notebook stays in the drawer.

Here's the one I didn't want to write. My wife, three weeks ago, was talking about a trip we might take next summer. Somewhere with a beach. She mentioned a number. A budget. I said sure, that sounds fine. And she said, and this is exact, 'We could actually afford a little more but I want to keep a cushion.' And I knew, in the way you know when you've hurt someone in a way that will not fully unhurt, that the cushion existed because of me. That we were budgeting the beach around the possibility that I might, at some point, in some quiet moment, do it again.

She didn't say that. She might not even have meant that. But that's what I heard. And that's what I'll hear for a while.

The cost of $1,528 in gambling losses is not $1,528. The cost is: a smaller trip in the summer. A glance at a phone at breakfast. A brother-in-law who won't quite look at me. A sister-in-law who won't quite look at my wife. A whole subset of the internet I don't visit. A pull at 10pm on a Tuesday. A notebook in a drawer.

And the guy with the anime profile picture is still posting. I don't read him anymore. I blocked him in November. But I know, because sometimes I hear other people quote him, that he is still there, still calling contracts mispriced, still posting frog memes at 6am when they crater.

He has 6,100 followers now. He's grown. Good for him. Whatever.

I mentioned in part three that the problem was never the stranger on the message board. The problem was I had no rows. No ledger. No record of what I'd acted on and what had happened. PredEve is the ledger I needed. Every signal gets logged. Every outcome gets recorded. You cannot go to predeve.com and find a claim without a row to back it up. The guy with the anime profile picture had 4,200 followers and zero rows. That is not the same as evidence. I paid $300 to learn that distinction. It's cheaper to learn it at predeve.com.

That's the tail.
