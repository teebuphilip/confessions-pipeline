# The $600 Line Move I Didn't See

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. This is mistake #4.*

---

The line moved four points and I didn't look once.

That's the whole story. You can stop here if you want. Everything else is me trying to explain how a grown man with a mortgage and a checking account and a functioning prefrontal cortex managed to give away six hundred dollars because he couldn't be bothered to open an app on a Wednesday.

It was August 19th. Second week of the season. I placed the bet on Tuesday night, sitting on my couch with a beer that had gone warm and a bowl of pretzels I didn't want. I liked the home team minus 3. I had reasons. I always have reasons. Reasons are the part of gambling that lets you pretend it's chess.

I put down three hundred. In my head that was a small bet. In my checking account it was groceries for two weeks. But three hundred felt like a number a serious person would bet, so I bet it, and I told myself I would monitor the line all week and adjust if something changed.

Reader. I did not monitor the line.

By Wednesday I was thinking about work. By Thursday my brother-in-law called about the thing with his kid. By Friday I was mowing the lawn and telling myself I'd check the line after. By Saturday I had convinced myself that checking now would be neurotic, because I had already made my decision and a real bettor commits to his read.

A real bettor. Listen to me.

Sunday morning I opened the app to watch my bet the way a man watches his own funeral. And there, at the top of the screen, was the line. It wasn't minus 3 anymore. It was plus 1. The home team was now getting a point. My team.

A four-point swing.

If you don't bet, let me tell you what a four-point swing means. It means everyone with money and information had spent the week walking the other way from me. Sharps, syndicates, people with models that make my spreadsheet look like a crayon drawing — all of them had been quietly saying no, no, no, no, while I sat on my couch nodding yes.

The line is a conversation. I had stopped listening on Tuesday.

I watched the game anyway. You always watch. That's the sickness. My team was down 14 by halftime and I made a sandwich I couldn't taste and told my wife I was fine when she asked and I wasn't fine, I was doing math in my head about what six hundred dollars was in real things. It was the brakes on the Corolla. It was two months of the streaming stack I keep meaning to cancel. It was the dentist appointment I'd been putting off.

By the fourth quarter I wasn't rooting for my team. I was rooting for a specific backdoor cover that involved a garbage-time touchdown and a missed extra point. The mind of a gambler in a losing bet is a beautiful and terrible thing. It becomes a small engineer, building bridges out of toothpicks over a canyon.

The touchdown never came. The extra point was moot. I lost the bet, I lost the juice, I lost the six hundred, and I lost something worse — the ability to tell myself I was a disciplined bettor.

Because discipline isn't picking a side on Tuesday.

Discipline is what you do Wednesday through Sunday.
