# The Friday I Opened The App And Looked At The Wrong Thing

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 4 of 943 (part 3 of 7).*

I finally opened the book Friday night, and I still didn't see the line move.

I finally opened the book Friday night, and I still didn't see the line move.

This is the part of the story that embarrasses me most, because I can't blame it on not looking. I looked. I looked and I didn't see. Which is a whole other, worse kind of failure.

It was around 9. My wife was watching one of her shows, the one with the British detective who's always sad about something. I was on the couch pretending to read a book about the Roman Empire that a coworker had lent me two years ago. I had not made it past page 40. I was rereading the same paragraph about grain shipments and thinking about the bet.

I opened the account.

Here's what I did in the app. Follow me carefully because this is the exact geometry of the mistake. I opened the account. I tapped "My Bets." I saw my bet. Home team minus 3. There was my stake, three hundred, and the potential payout, which I had memorized. Green text where it needed to be green. Everything looked fine.

I closed the account.

Do you see it? I looked at my bet. I did not look at the line.

Those are two different acts. My bet was locked in at minus 3 the moment I placed it Tuesday. That number was never going to change on my screen. Home team minus 3, forever, until the game was played. My screen was a museum. It showed me what I had bought, not what the world had done to it since.

To see the line move I would have had to do one more thing. Back out of "My Bets." Tap the sport. Tap the game. Look at the live number. Compare. That's it. Three taps.

I didn't do the three taps.

And here's the honest reason, the reason I've been sitting with for weeks now. I didn't want to know. Somewhere underneath the top layer of my brain, some older, smarter, sadder layer knew that if I checked the current line, I might find out something I didn't want to find out. I might find out I was wrong. I might find out that the version of me from Tuesday, the version who had done the spreadsheet and hit place with such confidence, was already looking foolish by Friday.

So I looked at the frozen number. The number that could still be right. The Schrödinger's number, in the sealed box.

This is a pattern I have in my life. I check my bank account balance without looking at the transactions. I check my weight without looking at the trend. I check my email inbox without opening the ones from people I owe things to. I look at the surface of the thing I'm afraid of, confirm the surface exists, and close the account.

My wife glanced over. She said, you okay. I said, yeah, fine. She said, you're not reading. I said, I am. She said, you've been on that page for an hour. I said, it's dense.

It was not dense. I had read the paragraph nine times.

Around 9:30 I opened the account again. Same thing. My Bets. Green. Close. Around 10:15, one more time. Same routine. Museum, museum, museum. I did this three separate times on Friday night. Three separate opportunities to do three more taps. I did zero more taps.

By Friday at midnight the line was at minus 1. My screen still said minus 3. I went to bed feeling like a man who had checked on his position and found it acceptable.

Saturday I mowed. Saturday I watched college football. Saturday I opened the account twice and did the same museum walk. Never once did I look at the current number.

By Saturday night the line had crossed zero. The home team, my team, was now a slight underdog. In the language of the market, everyone had spent the week moving off the side I was on. And I had spent the week refusing to see it while telling myself I was staying on top of my bet.

Sunday morning I opened the account to watch the game.

And this time, at the top of the screen, the current line was too big to miss.

*Sunday morning. Plus 1. My team. Not minus 3. Plus 1. Let me tell you what my face did.*

The whole point of it is that when a line moves four points in four days, that movement is a signal, not noise. The market is telling you something. In 2022 I had no system that heard any of it. I was checking the wrong number every time I opened the account, feeling due diligence I had not performed.
