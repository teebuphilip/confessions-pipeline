# The Middle Was A Language Problem

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 6 of 943 (part 3 of 7).*

There was a stretch, roughly February through May, where I stopped calling it gambling.

There was a stretch, roughly February through May, where I stopped calling it gambling.

I called it a lot of other things. I called it "the project." I called it "the arb thing." I called it "my little research hobby," which is what I told my sister on the phone one Sunday when she asked what I'd been up to, because she asks that every Sunday and I have to have an answer that isn't "nothing."

Here's what a language problem does to you. It buys you time. If the thing you're doing has a name that isn't the true name, then the true name doesn't apply to you. I was not a gambler. Gamblers were the guys at the OTB on the corner of my old neighborhood, guys with racing forms folded four ways in their coat pockets, guys my father warned me about when I was nine. I was a man with a laptop and a thesis. Totally different animal.

The thesis was that sportsbooks and prediction markets should disagree, structurally, and that the disagreement itself was the opportunity. And here's the thing that makes me want to put my head through a wall now, months later, sober and reading back through my own notes. The thesis was correct. The thesis is still correct. The venues do disagree, structurally, for real reasons, and if you understood those reasons deeply enough, and had the capital and the speed and the discipline of a professional, there might be something there.

But none of that described me. I had a laptop, a spreadsheet, and a mug that said WORLD'S OKAYEST DAD, which my son gave me the last Father's Day before he moved.

I want to tell you about a Saturday in March, because it's the Saturday that should have ended it, and did not.

I had eleven positions open. Four on FanDuel, seven on Kalshi. I had them stretched across three sports and one election contract. The election contract was the tell, if I'd been paying attention. A man who cannot balance his own hedges on football games should not be adding a special election in a state he could not find on a map without help. But I had told myself the election was a diversifier, which is another word that was doing work it hadn't earned.

My phone was buzzing every few minutes with alerts from both apps. I had two windows open on the laptop and one on my old iPad, because I'd started using the iPad to watch the games while the laptop had the spreadsheet. The couch was covered in receipts I had printed for reasons I could not have explained to you. I had eaten a bowl of cereal for lunch at three in the afternoon standing over the sink.

A friend called. He'd been calling for weeks. His wife had had a scare in January and I had meant to check in and had not, because I had been busy with the project. When his name came up on the phone I looked at it for maybe eight seconds and then I hit decline. I hit decline. On a friend whose wife had had a scare. Because a game was in the third quarter and a line was moving.

I want to be really careful how I say this next part. I did not feel bad in the moment. I felt annoyed. I felt like he was calling at a bad time. I felt, and this is the part I don't like admitting, like his call was interrupting something important.

The game ended. I lost, on net, about a hundred and forty bucks across the eleven positions. I called him back at ten that night. He didn't pick up. He texted me the next morning to say his wife was okay and thanks for reaching out. He used the word "reaching" like a man being polite about the fact that I hadn't.

I did not stop after that Saturday. I want to say I did. I want to give you a clean scene where a man looks at his own reflection in the black screen of a phone and has a moment of clarity. That scene did not happen. What happened is I updated the spreadsheet. I moved eight hundred dollars from savings into the FanDuel account to "give the hedges more room." I made a plan to review my methodology on Sunday. I did not review my methodology on Sunday. I placed three more bets.

The language of it kept me safe from the fact of it for four more months. And when the fact of it finally caught up, it did not do so gently.
