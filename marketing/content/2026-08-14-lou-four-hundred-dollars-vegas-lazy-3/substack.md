# How I Explained It To Myself So I Could Keep Going

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 3 of 943 (part 3 of 7).*

By February I had a whole philosophy.

By February I had a whole philosophy.

That's the dangerous part. Not the bets. The philosophy. If you ever catch yourself building a philosophy around a habit, the habit has already won.

Mine went like this. Sports betting was 'entertainment.' The money I bet was 'money I would have spent on other things anyway.' The wins were 'proof of process.' The losses were 'variance.' The time spent was 'a hobby, like golf.' The Twitter accounts I followed were 'research.' The spreadsheet was 'tracking.' The 4am wake-ups to check overnight Australian rules football were 'international diversification.'

I want you to sit with that last one for a second. I set an alarm for 4am to check the score of a sport I had never watched in person, played by men from a country I had never visited, on a field I could not have described if you paid me. And I called it international diversification. Out loud. To myself. In my own kitchen. With a straight face.

My wife had, by this point, moved past the list. The list phase had ended sometime in January. Now she was in what I later understood to be the observation phase. She wasn't commenting. She wasn't asking. She was just watching. If you have ever been watched by someone who is deciding something about you without telling you, you know the specific quality of that quiet.

Here's a Saturday morning in February. I want to give you the whole scene because this is the one that still visits me at 3am sometimes.

I was at the kitchen table with the laptop open. Coffee. The good coffee, the kind she buys, not the freeze-dried garbage I buy for weekdays. She was at the counter making eggs. There was a game on that afternoon I had been researching for two days. I use the word researching loosely. I had watched clips. I had read a preview on a website with three pop-up ads for penis pills. I had checked the weather in the host city. Thirty-eight and drizzle. I had decided drizzle was good for the under.

She put a plate in front of me. Eggs, toast, one slice of tomato. She always does the tomato. She sat down across from me. She said, 'How much are you up.'

Not a question. A statement with a question mark implied but not delivered.

I said, 'For the year? Or since I started?'

She said, 'Total.'

I knew the answer to the dollar. I lied. I said 'a few hundred.'

The actual number, since August, was minus $340. I had lost the initial $400, won some back through fall, given it back through January, and was currently down $340 heading into a February I was very confident about. I told her 'a few hundred' and I made it sound like it was a positive few hundred. I let her believe I was up. I did that on purpose. I heard myself do it and I kept doing it.

She nodded. She ate her eggs. She said, 'Okay.'

That was it. That was the whole conversation. She didn't push. She didn't ask to see the account. She didn't demand a spreadsheet. She said okay and she ate her eggs and she went to her mother's for the afternoon.

I told myself she believed me. I told myself she was fine with it. I told myself the conversation went well.

What she was actually doing, I would find out later, was giving me one last chance to be honest without being forced. And I had failed it. She knew. She had checked the joint account. She had seen the transfers. She had done the math while I was in the shower on a Wednesday. She just wanted to hear what I would say when she asked.

I said a few hundred. Up.

And I bet the game that afternoon. And I lost another $180.

And the philosophy held. In my head, that Saturday, the philosophy held. Entertainment. Variance. Research. Diversification.

The philosophy is the last thing to die. Longer than the money. Longer than the marriage, in some cases. The philosophy is the thing you're still whispering to yourself while the house is on fire.

Tomorrow the fire.

*The night I bet the mortgage payment I had already been given.*

— Lou
