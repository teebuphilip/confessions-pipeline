# The Cowardly Crawl Out

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 5 of 943 (part 5 of 7).*

The first thing I did the morning after was not delete the app, and I want to explain why that matters.

I thought about it. I woke up at 6:20 AM after not sleeping, and I stared at the icon on my phone for maybe two minutes, and I moved my thumb over it in that way you do when you're about to press-and-hold to get the delete option. And I did not press. I put the phone down.

The reason I did not delete it — and this is the first cowardly thing I did, of many, in a sequence I'm going to lay out for you honestly — is that I wanted to leave myself an out. I wanted the option. Not the plan. Just the option. In the same way a smoker who is quitting keeps one pack in a drawer they promise they'll never open, I kept the app on my phone so that I could tell myself I was choosing not to use it, rather than not having it to use.

Coward move number one. Logged.

Coward move number two was telling my brother a shaped version of the truth. I called him at 9 AM. I told him I had lost "a lot." I did not tell him $9,796. I told him "most of the bathroom money." He asked what most meant. I said "more than half." It was not more than half. It was 89%. I let him think it was 55%. I let him give me a lecture that was calibrated to a 55% loss instead of an 89% loss. The lecture I got was gentler than the one I deserved, and I received it gratefully, and I hung up the phone feeling like I had confessed when what I had actually done was auditioned a confession.

Coward move number three was not telling my accountant. I have an accountant. She's been doing my taxes for eleven years. Her name is not important. What is important is that she has a category on her intake form for "unusual income or losses over $1,000" and I have never once in eleven years lied on that form and this year I did. I checked no. I checked no on a form where the honest answer was a paragraph.

Coward move number four was moving money around to hide the hole. I had a Roth. I took a distribution I shouldn't have taken. I moved it into checking. I told myself it was for the bathroom renovation, which was true in the sense that it went into the account the bathroom renovation was supposed to come out of, and false in every other sense that matters.

Coward move number five was the search history I cleared. I had spent six weeks Googling things like "is 3000 a lot to lose on prediction markets" and "how do sharps identify soft opens" and "kalshi vs polymarket for beginners" and "can you write off gambling losses on prediction markets." I cleared all of it. Not because anyone was going to look. Because I wanted the trail to stop existing.

Coward move number six was the worst one. I bet again. Small. $60. Two weeks after the bathroom floor. On a market I told myself I understood. It resolved in my favor. I made $47. I felt, for about four hours, like I had been overreacting the whole time. Like maybe the bathroom floor had been dramatic. Like maybe I was one of those guys who could do this if he just kept the size down. That $47 was more expensive than any of the losses. It was expensive because it argued with everything I had learned. It made a case for the guy I was trying to stop being.

Coward move number seven was not going to a meeting. There's a Gamblers Anonymous meeting eleven minutes from my house. I looked it up. I put it in my calendar. I moved it three times. I never went. I have still, at the time of writing this, never gone. I'm not proud of that. I'm also not going to pretend I've gone.

What I did do was set up a hard block on the app. Not delete it — remember, coward move number one, I wanted the option. But a block. A screen time limit at the OS level, with a password I had my brother set, which I did not tell him what it was for. That was the closest thing to a real move I made in the crawl out. Everything else was theater.

The crawl out took about eleven weeks. At the end of it I was not fixed. I was contained. There is a difference, and I don't want to overstate the difference. Contained means the fire is not spreading. It does not mean the fire is out. It means I have not placed a bet in ninety-four days as of writing this, and that if you asked me whether I was cured I would laugh in your face.

I am not cured. I am waiting.
