# What I Should Have Done

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 7 of 943 (part 6 of 7).*

No metaphors in this one. Rules only. If you bet, these are the rules I wish someone had handed me on a piece of paper in 1994.

No metaphors in this one. Rules only. If you bet, these are the rules I wish someone had handed me on a piece of paper in 1994.

Some of these are about betting. Most are about being a person who bets. If you don't bet, most of them still apply — replace "bet" with whatever your thing is. Trading. Drinking. Ex-girlfriends. Doesn't matter. The machinery is the same.

1. Never open the account the day after a loss. Not to check. Not to look. Not to "see the balance." The 12:14pm click at your desk is the whole game. Once the app is open, you have already lost the next fight, you just don't know it yet. The rule is 48 hours minimum after any loss. Not a suggestion. A rule.

2. Set the number before you sit down. Before, not during. "I will bet $40 on this game tonight." Written down, ideally, or spoken out loud to another human. Any bet placed above that number, for any reason, including a "lock," including insider info from Dwayne who chews pens, is a chase. Full stop.

3. If you use the word "thesis" about a bet, you are not being professional. You are lying. Pros don't call their picks a thesis. Pros call them plays. "Thesis" is what people say when they're building a permission slip. Kill the word.

4. Never live-bet after a loss the same night. Ever. The live-betting menu after a losing bet is a slot machine. It exists to catch you in your worst emotional state and drain you fast. If you have lost a bet tonight, the live-bet tab is closed for you. This is non-negotiable.

5. No spreadsheets after 9pm. If you are opening Excel to calculate how to get back to a starting number, you are not gambling. You are performing math in service of an emotion. Close the laptop. Eat a piece of bread. Go to bed.

6. Self-exclude from all apps, not one. If you only self-exclude from one, you are keeping a spare. You are lying to yourself and you know it. All of them or none of them.

7. Tell someone the real number. Not the rounded-down number. Not the "about" number. The real number. Say the actual dollar amount out loud to another person who loves you. The moment you say it out loud, the number stops running the show. This is the single most useful thing I ever did and I did it years too late.

8. If a therapist asks you a real question, answer it. Do not email him three days later saying you're traveling for work. He knows you're not traveling for work. You know you're not traveling for work. Show up next week and answer the question, even badly. Especially badly.

9. Any bet placed to "get back to even" is a losing bet before the ball is snapped. Not because of edge. Because of motive. A bet placed to feel a certain way is a bet you cannot win, because even if it hits, it teaches your brain to do it again. See rule 10.

10. Winning is more dangerous than losing when you're chasing. If you press a bad decision and it hits, the correct response is not celebration. The correct response is fear. The universe just paid your bad instincts. Withdraw the money. Delete the app. Sit in the dark for ten minutes and understand that you got away with something. Getting away with something is not the same as being safe.

11. There is no such thing as house money. Every dollar in the account is your dollar. The dollar you "won" last week is the same dollar as the dollar you earned at work. If you wouldn't bet your paycheck on it, you shouldn't bet your winnings on it.

12. Do not bet sober alone at night in an apartment. Not because sober is bad. Because sober alone at night in an apartment is the exact chemical setup for the closed loop. You need friction. Bet with a friend at a bar if you must bet, so that the shame is public and the amounts are small. Isolation is the accelerant.

13. Call your mother on her birthday. This one is not about betting. But if the reason you don't call her is because of a bet, that's the moment to stop. That's the actual moment. That is the sign that you have chosen a screen over a person, and if you don't correct it now you will correct it at her funeral, and by then it will not be a correction, it will be a scar.

14. "Currently not doing it" is a valid state. You do not have to be recovered. You do not have to have beaten it. You have to have not done it today. That's the whole assignment. Today. Not the week. Not the month. Not the arc. Today.

15. Write down the specific dollar amounts of your worst nights. Not to punish yourself. To remember. When you're tempted, read the list. The list is the antidote. My list starts with $150 in 30 hours on a Wednesday in September. It gets worse from there. Yours will too, if you keep one. Keep one.

That's the playbook. Fifteen rules. None of them are hard to understand. All of them are hard to follow.

I have followed them, on average, about 60% of the time since I wrote them down. Which means 40% of the time I have failed. And that's a man who has been trying, in bad faith and good, for a decade.

Do better than 60%. Start earlier than I did.

*Last post is the tail. What this still costs me, every month, ten years on.*

— Lou
