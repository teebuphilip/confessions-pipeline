# What I Should Have Done

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 5 of 943 (part 6 of 7).*

If you are going to do this — and I hope you won't — here is what I should have done, in the order I should have done it.

If you are going to do this — and I hope you won't — here is what I should have done, in the order I should have done it. No metaphors. No stories. Just the moves. I paid roughly $10,000 in tuition for these. They are free to you.

One. Do not bet during a soft open. Ever. If the market opened within the last two hours, you are looking at a placeholder price. The number on the screen was generated to have a number on the screen. Wait for volume. Wait for the price to move at least once on real transactions. If you cannot tell the difference between real transactions and platform-seeded liquidity, you are not ready to bet on this platform. That is not a moral failing. That is just information.

Two. Check at least two venues before entering. If the book and another shop disagree by more than five cents on the same event, one of them is wrong, and it is not you who knows which one. The disagreement is not your opportunity. The disagreement is information about which venue has caught up to reality. Wait for them to converge within five cents. Then enter, if you must enter at all, on the side with more volume.

Three. Set a session cap before you open the account. Not a monthly cap. Not a weekly cap. A session cap. The number you are willing to lose today, right now, this sitting. When you hit it, close the account. Do not open it again for 24 hours. This is the rule that will save you the most money and it is the rule you will break first.

Four. Never enter after 10 PM. The 4 AM version of you is not making the bet. The 10 PM version is. The 10 PM version is tired, is bored, is looking for something. Nothing you enter after 10 PM will be your best work. Move your app to a folder on the last screen of your phone. Better: delete it every night and reinstall it in the morning. Yes, that is annoying. That is the point.

Five. Do not chase. If you lose, close the account. Do not, under any circumstances, try to "make it back" in the same session. The math on chasing is bad. The math on chasing while emotional is worse. The math on chasing while emotional at 10:40 PM is not math, it is a suicide pact with your bank account.

Six. Do not build a spreadsheet you will lie in. If you are tracking, track through the platform's own reporting. Take screenshots of the balance, not of the wins. If you cannot look at your bank account and your platform balance together without flinching, you are already in trouble, and no spreadsheet will save you. Spreadsheets are for people who want to know. If you are building one, ask yourself, honestly, if that is what you want.

Seven. Tell one person the actual number. Not a shaped version. The actual number. Pick the person whose disappointment you fear the most and tell them the number to the dollar. This is not a therapy exercise. This is a control mechanism. The number will feel different once someone else knows it.

Eight. Do not "test" whether you can bet small responsibly. You cannot. I mean you specifically, the person reading this, who has read this far because something in this arc sounded familiar. You are not the exception. There is no version of "just $60" that stays $60. The $47 I won on the small test bet cost me more than any of the losses. Do not run that experiment.

Nine. Put a block on the account at the OS level, with a password someone else sets. Not you. Not your spouse, if your spouse is the person you are hiding losses from. A brother, a friend, an accountant. Someone whose willingness to give you the password can be counted on to be inconvenient.

Ten. If you have already lost more than you can honestly explain to the person who shares your finances, stop reading this post and go tell them. Not tomorrow. Today. Every day you wait, the number of the confession grows and the shape of the confession changes. The best version of the confession is the one that happens the soonest.

Eleven. Go to a meeting. I have not done this. I am telling you to do this. The fact that I have not done it and am telling you to do it is exactly why you should do it. Do not be me.

Twelve. Assume you are not cured. Assume you are contained. Behave accordingly.

That's the list. That's what six weeks and $9,796 taught me. I would like to say I follow all twelve. I follow ten of them. Guess which two I don't.

— Lou
