# The $300 Vibe Check

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. This is mistake #8.*

---

There's a specific kind of stupid that only shows up after 50. It's not the loud kind. Not the young-guy-in-a-Camaro kind. It's quieter. It's a man in a bathrobe at 11pm on a Sunday, reading a tweet from someone named @EdgeLord_Alpha, and deciding this stranger knows something he doesn't.

That was me on September 16th.

I was on Kalshi. If you don't know what Kalshi is, congratulations, you're doing better than me. It's a prediction market. You bet on things. Elections. Weather. Whether some CEO will get fired by Friday. It's legal gambling that dresses up in a lab coat and calls itself 'event contracts.'

I had $312 in the account. I was up $9 for the month. Nine dollars. I remember because I was proud of it in a way that would embarrass me if I said it out loud.

Then I saw the tweet.

'Kalshi is mispriced on Sunday. If you know, you know.'

That's it. That's the whole tweet. Sixty-two characters. No chart. No source. No explanation of what was mispriced or why. Just a guy with an anime profile picture and 4,200 followers announcing, essentially, that the market was wrong and he alone had noticed.

And I believed him.

I want to be clear about something. I have never met this person. I don't know his name. I don't know if he's ever made money on Kalshi or if he's a 19-year-old in Ohio typing with Cheeto dust on his fingers. I know nothing. But the word 'sharp' had been floating around my feed for weeks, and 'sharp' sounded like someone who knew things, and I did not know things, and I wanted very badly to know things.

So I clicked over to Kalshi. Found a contract I barely understood. Something about a Sunday economic indicator. Put in $300.

My wife was watching a British baking show in the next room. I could hear the tent flapping.

I clicked confirm.

Here's the part I still can't explain. I felt calm. Not excited. Not scared. Calm. Because I had outsourced the decision. It wasn't my bet anymore. It was EdgeLord_Alpha's bet. If it went south, it wasn't my fault. I was just following the sharp.

I went to bed at midnight, up 4% on paper. I remember thinking maybe this was it. Maybe I'd finally figured something out at 55. Maybe the sharps were real and I was one tweet away from joining them.

I woke up at 6am and checked my phone before I peed.

The number was red.
