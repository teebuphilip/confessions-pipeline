# The Sunday She Found The Statements

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 3 of 943 (part 4 of 7).*

The statements were in a manila folder in the third drawer of the filing cabinet in the basement.

The statements were in a manila folder in the third drawer of the filing cabinet in the basement.

I want to tell you why I kept paper statements for an online betting account. I don't know. I don't have an answer for you. There is a version of me that believed if I printed them out and put them in a folder marked 'HVAC WARRANTIES,' I was somehow being organized. Adult. In control. Look at me, keeping records. Look at me, taking this seriously.

The folder said HVAC WARRANTIES on the tab. In my own handwriting. In blue Sharpie. That is the level of premeditated concealment we are dealing with here. A grown man in the year of our Lord printing PDFs of losing bets and hiding them behind a lie about heating equipment.

March. A Sunday. Around 2pm. She was looking for a warranty on the water heater because we had a leak on the pilot side and she wanted to see if it was still covered. She went to the filing cabinet. She opened the third drawer. She saw HVAC WARRANTIES. She pulled it.

Inside the folder were sixteen months of statements. From two accounts I had told her about, and one she didn't know existed.

I was upstairs watching basketball. The kind of basketball where I had money on the total. Over 218.5. I remember the number because when she came up the stairs I was doing the mental math on where we needed to be at halftime for it to hit.

She walked into the living room holding the folder open. She didn't say anything at first. She stood in the doorway with the folder open in both hands like she was carrying a small dead animal she wanted me to identify.

She said, 'What is this.'

I said, 'It's the betting stuff. I told you about the betting.'

She said, 'You told me about one account.'

Silence.

Here's where I want to freeze the tape and tell you what was going through my head, because I think it's important. I was not thinking, oh no, I have hurt my wife. I was not thinking, I have broken her trust. I was thinking: how do I get out of this conversation and still get to watch the second half. That was the actual thought. That was the actual sequence of neurons firing in my actual skull.

A man's worst self is not who he is when he's angry. A man's worst self is who he is when he's about to be caught and doesn't want to miss the game.

She sat down on the arm of the couch. Not the couch. The arm. She has never sat on the arm of the couch in nineteen years. It's a small thing. It felt like she was hovering above the room, ready to leave.

She said, 'The third account. The one at the second book. That's from six months ago. October.'

I said, 'Yeah.'

She said, 'The balance was $2,200 in October.'

I said, 'Yeah.'

She said, 'It's $180 now.'

I said nothing.

She said, 'Where did the two thousand dollars go, Lou.'

And here is the moment. Here is the specific moment I want you to have. I could have told her the truth. The truth was I had lost it. All of it. Over about eleven weeks. In small dumb bets across three sports, most of them games I couldn't have picked the teams for out of a lineup. I could have told her that.

Instead I said, 'Some of it went to Christmas.'

Some of it went to Christmas.

I want that engraved on my headstone as a warning to other men. Here lies Lou. Some of it went to Christmas.

She stood up. She said, 'The Christmas budget was $600. I know because I did it. I did the whole Christmas budget by myself while you were on your phone.'

She walked out of the room with the folder.

She went to the kitchen. I heard her open the drawer where we keep takeout menus and old batteries. I heard her put the folder in there. Then she came back to the living room, walked past me, went upstairs, and shut the bedroom door.

She didn't slam it.

The game went under.

I lost the bet.

And I sat on the couch and watched the fourth quarter of a basketball game I no longer had any reason to watch, because I couldn't move, because I didn't know what to do, because there is a specific paralysis that hits a man when his wife has stopped raising her voice and started filing things.

Tomorrow: what I did on Monday morning, which was mostly cowardice.

*I made an appointment I didn't intend to keep and told her I did.*

— Lou
