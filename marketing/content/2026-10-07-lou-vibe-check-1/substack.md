# the post That Cost Me $300

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 8 of 943 (part 1 of 7).*

It was 11:47pm on a Sunday and I was about to hand $300 to a man with an anime profile picture.

It was 11:47pm on a Sunday and I was about to hand $300 to a man with an anime profile picture.

I didn't know it yet. That's the thing about handing money to strangers. You never know you're doing it. You think you're making a decision.

Let me set the scene. My wife was in the living room watching some British baking show where they cry over undercooked tarts. I was in the kitchen, laptop open, one leg tucked under me on the chair because I'd read somewhere that sitting like that was bad for your hip and I liked doing it anyway. Small rebellion. The kind you have at 55.

I had a account with the book. If you don't know the book, don't learn. It's a website where you bet real dollars on whether things will happen. Elections. Fed rates. Whether some hurricane will make landfall in a specific county by Tuesday. They call the bets 'contracts.' The word 'contract' makes it sound legitimate. Like you're at a desk. Like there's a lawyer somewhere.

There is not a lawyer somewhere.

I had $312 in the account. I know because I remember the number exactly. I had put in $500 four months earlier and I was up $9 for September. Nine dollars. I want to say that number again because it matters. Nine dollars. I was proud of the nine dollars in the way a man is proud of nothing else in his life so he settles on nine dollars from a gambling website.

I was scrolling a message board. Not looking for anything. That's how it always starts. You're not looking. You're just scrolling. Waiting for your brain to give up so you can sleep.

And then I saw it.

'line is mispriced on Sunday. If you know, you know.'

Sixty-two characters. Anime avatar. Handle was something like EdgeLord_Alpha, I don't remember exactly, and I'm not going back to look because I don't want to give him the click. He had 4,200 followers. A blue check. Some bio about being a 'sharp' who runs a 'model.' No mention of what the model models. No screenshots of returns. Nothing.

I stared at that post for maybe two minutes.

Here's what I want you to understand. I have a business degree. I ran a small operation for eleven years. I have read approximately four books about behavioral finance. I know, in the way that you know your name, that anonymous message board accounts telling you a market is mispriced are lying or wrong or both.

I knew it.

And I clicked over to the book anyway.

The market he was talking about — I figured out which one from context in the replies — was a Sunday-close contract on some economic indicator I'd never traded before. I didn't understand the underlying. I didn't understand why it might be mispriced. I saw the 'yes' side was at 42 cents. I saw a lot of volume. I saw people in the replies saying 'in' with fire emojis.

I put in $300.

I did not stop to check my logic because I did not have logic. I had a post. I had a stranger. I had the word 'sharp' rattling around in my brain like a coin in a dryer.

I hit confirm. My hand was steady. That's what I remember most. My hand was steady because I had already decided this wasn't my decision. It was EdgeLord's. If it worked, I was smart. If it didn't, he was wrong. Beautiful little accounting trick your brain does when you're too tired to be a person.

I closed the laptop. I brushed my teeth. My wife asked what I was up to. I said 'nothing.' First lie of the night. Not the last.

I went to bed thinking maybe, just maybe, I had finally figured something out.

Tomorrow I'll tell you what I woke up to.

*Tomorrow: the morning after, and the number in red.*

— Lou
