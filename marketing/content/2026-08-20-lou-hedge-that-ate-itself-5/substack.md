# Every Coward Move

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 6 of 943 (part 5 of 7).*

I want to be honest about how I crawled out of this, which means I have to tell you about the moves I'm not proud of, and there were a lot of them.

I want to be honest about how I crawled out of this, which means I have to tell you about the moves I'm not proud of, and there were a lot of them.

The Sunday in June did not make me quit. That's the first coward move. In the movie version of my life, the Sunday makes me quit. In the real version, I went two weeks without placing a bet and then placed a small one on a Monday night football game because I told myself I needed to "stay in the flow." That bet lost. So did the next one. By the end of July I was down another twelve hundred and I had not told anybody anything.

Coward move number two: I lied to my sister. She called in July and asked if I was okay because I'd been weird on the phone, and I said I was fine, work was busy, which is a hilarious thing for me to have said because I work from home doing a job I do not want to describe here and it was, at that moment, extremely not busy. She didn't push. She never pushes. She's been not-pushing for about forty years and I have taken advantage of it my whole life.

Coward move number three: I opened a third account. This one on a smaller offshore book. I told myself it was because they had better lines on the specific kind of hedge I was trying to run. What it actually was, and I knew this at the time, was that I had a running mental tally of how much I was down on FanDuel and Kalshi, and if I lost on a third platform, that money wouldn't count against my main tally. I would like to tell you I don't remember thinking this. I remember thinking this. I remember thinking it while brushing my teeth.

Coward move number four: I moved money out of an IRA. Not a huge amount. Twenty-two hundred. Took the tax hit and everything. I told myself it was because I needed a buffer in checking. The buffer went into checking on a Tuesday and was in one of the three betting accounts by Friday. I have not told anyone about the IRA. Not my sister. Not the friend whose call I let go to voicemail. My accountant knows because my accountant does my taxes and numbers don't lie, and when we did my return in the spring he asked me one question about it and I said something vague and he wrote it down and moved on. I love him for that. He is a good man doing a hard job for a bad client.

The crawl out actually started on a Wednesday in August. Not with a scene. Not with a moment. With an email.

The email was from FanDuel. It was a "responsible gaming" check-in, triggered automatically because of some threshold I had crossed in my deposit pattern. It was a form email. It had a link to some resources. It was, in every meaningful way, an ignorable thing.

I did not ignore it. I don't know why. I had ignored a thousand more direct signals over the previous ten months. But that email, that particular Wednesday, sitting between a Costco receipt and a spam email about extended car warranties, made me sit down at the kitchen table and put my head in my hands and stay there for about twenty minutes.

I did not click the link in the email. That would have been too dignified. What I did was I opened both apps and set deposit limits on each of them. Low ones. Not zero. I couldn't do zero. Zero felt like a admission and I was still not ready to admit. But I set them low enough that the way I had been running things wasn't possible anymore.

Then I called my sister. Not to tell her the whole thing. I told her a small version of it. I said I had been messing with some betting stuff and had lost some money and I wanted her to know because I wanted somebody to know. She said okay. She asked how much. I said less than it was, but not by a huge amount. She didn't say much else. She said, "Are you okay right now," and I said, "I think so," and she said, "Call me tomorrow," and I did.

Coward move number five, and this is the last one for today, is that I still have not closed the accounts. I have not deposited into them in months. I have not placed a bet in months. But the accounts are still open. There is a small balance on Kalshi, twenty-three dollars, that I have not withdrawn because withdrawing it would require me to log in and I do not want to log in.

Some part of me is holding a door open. I know this. I am telling you because saying it out loud on the internet is, apparently, the only way I know how to make things real.
