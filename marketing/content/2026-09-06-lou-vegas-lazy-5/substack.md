# Every Coward Move I Made In The Next Ninety Days

*I've made 943 serious mistakes in my life. I'm documenting every one of them so you don't have to make the same ones. My name is Lou Zerr. Here's another 3 of 943 (part 5 of 7).*

Monday morning I told her I made an appointment with a counselor. I had not made an appointment with a counselor.

Monday morning I told her I made an appointment with a counselor. I had not made an appointment with a counselor.

I want to lead with that because if I lead with the other stuff you'll think there was a moment of grace in here somewhere. There wasn't. Not for a while. The first ninety days after she found the folder were, without exaggeration, the most cowardly ninety days of my adult life, and I say that as a man who once hid in a Denny's bathroom for forty minutes to avoid a difficult conversation with a girlfriend in 1994.

Move one: the fake appointment. Monday morning I came downstairs in a dress shirt like a man who had things handled. I told her I had called someone. I said I had a Thursday appointment. I said his name was Dr. Morrow, which was a name I made up on the spot from a mattress store I had driven past on the way home from Home Depot the previous week. Morrow Mattress. I gave a therapist the name of a mattress company. She said 'okay.' She did not say good. She did not say thank you. She said okay.

Move two: the actual phone call, which happened Wednesday, because guilt is a very slow-acting drug. I called a number I found on my insurance website. I left a voicemail. I hung up. I texted her a screenshot of the outgoing call. I did not tell her it was a voicemail. I let the screenshot do the lying for me. Passive lying. The most cowardly kind. Lying by allowing an image to imply something that wasn't true.

Move three: the app. I did not delete it. I moved it into a folder labeled 'Utilities' on the third home screen of my phone. Behind the flashlight. If you ever want to know where a man is hiding something on his phone, look behind the flashlight. Nobody uses the flashlight. Nobody looks behind it.

Move four: the small bet. Six days after she found the folder, I bet forty dollars on a hockey game. Forty dollars. I want you to hear how small that is and I want you to hear how big it was. It was small enough that I told myself it didn't count. It was big enough that when I hit confirm my hands were shaking. If your hands are shaking, it counts. That's the rule. That was the rule I made up later and it's the only good rule I've ever made.

Move five: the therapist, actually. Two weeks in. A woman named Diane with a small office above a nail salon. I went because I couldn't figure out how to fake it anymore. She asked me why I was there. I said 'my wife found the statements.' She said 'so you're here for your wife.' I said 'yes.' She said 'that's not going to work.' I sat there for the rest of the fifty minutes not really talking and I paid her $170 out of pocket because I didn't want it on the insurance because I didn't want my wife to see the EOB.

Cowardice on cowardice on cowardice. Cowardice all the way down.

Move six: the confession that wasn't. Around day thirty I sat her down and told her I had 'slipped' with a small bet. I said 'slipped' like it was ice on a sidewalk. Like I hadn't planned it. Like it hadn't been the only thing I thought about for three days. She said 'how much.' I said forty. She said 'okay.' Again with the okay.

What I didn't tell her, that night, was that I had also bet on Wednesday. And the previous Sunday. Three bets, not one. I told her about the biggest of the three and I let it stand in for all of them.

A partial confession is a full lie in a nicer shirt.

Move seven, day forty-something: I finally opened the account. For the first time. Ever. Not to bet. Just to look. And what I saw made me sit down on the edge of the bed and put my face in my hands.

Because the number I had bet on that August Tuesday, the minus 3.5 that became minus 1, had been priced with the book at minus 1.5 the entire time. The market had known. Regular people, with regular money, using a regular website I had never opened, had priced the game correctly while I was buying a garden hose.

Eight months late. I finally opened the tab.

Move eight was telling my wife about all of it. Every bet since the folder. The three not the one. The forty and the sixty and the twenty. I did it on a Tuesday night, in the kitchen, standing up, because I was afraid if I sat down I'd stop talking. She listened. She didn't cry. She didn't yell. She said, 'Thank you for telling me.' And she went to bed.

We didn't sleep in the same room for eleven nights after that. Not because she kicked me out. Because I couldn't look at her.

Move nine, and the last one I'll tell you about here, was small and it was the one that mattered. I went to Diane. I told her I was there for me this time. She said 'okay, now we can start.'

We started.

We are still going.

Tomorrow: the actual rules I use now. No metaphors. No inspiration. Just the specific things I do so I don't become the guy on the couch watching a game he no longer had any reason to watch.

*The rules are boring on purpose. Boring is what saved me.*

— Lou
